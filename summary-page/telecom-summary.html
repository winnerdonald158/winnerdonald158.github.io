Project Overview

Customer churn is an important challenge for telecom companies because losing existing customers can affect recurring revenue and increase the need to acquire new customers.

In this project, I used PostgreSQL and Power BI to analyze 7,043 telecom customer records and understand where churn is concentrated, why customers reported leaving, when churn is most common, and which customer characteristics are associated with higher churn.

I broke the analysis into five business questions covering churn severity, reported churn reasons, customer segments, tenure, payment methods, and service adoption.

Goal

To understand the main patterns behind customer churn and identify customer groups that may need greater attention when planning retention efforts.

Process

  • I prepared and explored the telecom customer data before analysis.
  • I used PostgreSQL to calculate the overall churn rate and investigate customer churn categories and reported reasons.
  • I compared churn across contract type and internet type to identify higher-risk customer segments.
  • I analyzed how churn changes across different customer tenure groups.
  • I compared stayed and churned customers across contract type, payment method, Online Security, and Online Backup.
  • I identified the findings that were most relevant to customer retention and presented them through a Power BI dashboard.
  • I translated the findings into recommendations focused on customer retention, onboarding, service adoption, and customer value.

Key Insights

  • 26.54% of customers had churned. 1,869 out of 7,043 customers were classified as churned, meaning roughly 1 in 4 customers had left.
  • Competitor-related churn was the largest category. The Competitor category accounted for approximately 45% of churned customers, with reported reasons including better devices, better offers, more data, and higher download speeds.
  • Month-to-month customers had the highest churn rates. This pattern remained across the different internet types analyzed.
  • Newer customers were more likely to churn. Churn generally decreased as customer tenure increased, with shorter-tenure customers showing higher churn rates.
  • Month-to-month customers showed higher churn proportions. Customers on longer-term contracts, particularly two-year contracts, showed higher proportions of customers who stayed.
  • Mailed-check customers showed a higher churn proportion. Customers using credit cards showed a higher proportion of customers who stayed compared with the other payment methods analyzed.
  • Service adoption differed between stayed and churned customers. Customers without Online Security and Online Backup showed higher churn proportions than customers who subscribed to these services.

Key Retention Opportunities

  • Review competitor offerings, pricing, devices, and service benefits to understand where customers perceive greater value and identify areas where the company's own offering can be strengthened.
  • Give greater retention attention to month-to-month customers and other higher-risk customer groups.
  • Strengthen onboarding and early customer engagement for newer customers.
  • Clearly communicate the benefits of Online Security, Online Backup, and other key services during onboarding and encourage adoption where appropriate.
  • Monitor customer groups with higher churn proportions by payment method and other customer characteristics.
