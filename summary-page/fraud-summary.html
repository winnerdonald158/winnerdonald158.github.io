Project Overview

Fraudulent transactions can create financial losses and increase risk for businesses. Understanding where fraudulent activity is concentrated can help businesses prioritize monitoring and investigation.

In this project, I used PostgreSQL and Power BI to analyze 594,643 transaction records and investigate patterns associated with fraudulent activity across transaction categories, merchants, customers, and transaction-value ranges.

I broke the analysis into five business questions to understand the scale of fraud, where fraudulent activity is concentrated, which merchants and customers are associated with repeated activity, and how fraud risk changes with transaction amount.

Goal

To understand the main patterns in fraudulent transaction activity, identify areas of higher fraud risk, and provide actionable recommendations that can help prioritize fraud monitoring and investigation.

Process

  • I prepared and explored the fraud transaction dataset before analysis.
  • I used PostgreSQL to calculate the overall fraud rate and fraudulent transaction value.
  • I analyzed fraudulent activity across transaction categories using fraud volume, fraud rate, and fraudulent transaction value.
  • I examined merchant-level fraud activity to identify merchants associated with unusually high fraud rates, volumes, and transaction values.
  • I investigated customers associated with repeated transactions labeled as fraudulent.
  • I analyzed transaction-value ranges to understand how fraud rates change as transaction amounts increase.
  • I translated the findings into recommendations focused on fraud monitoring, screening, and investigation.
  • I am using the findings to develop a Power BI dashboard that communicates the main fraud-risk patterns clearly.

Key Insights

  • 7,200 transactions were labeled fraudulent. Out of 594,643 transactions, fraudulent transactions represented approximately 1.21% of all transactions.
  • Approximately $3.82M was associated with fraudulent transactions. This provides an indication of the financial value represented by transactions labeled fraudulent in the dataset.
  • Fraud risk differs across transaction categories. Leisure recorded the highest fraud rate, Travel had the highest fraudulent transaction value, while Sports & Toys had the highest number of fraudulent transactions.
  • Fraud activity is concentrated across certain merchants. Different merchants showed different combinations of fraud volume, fraud rate, and fraudulent transaction value.
  • Some customers were associated with repeated fraudulent activity. Repeated activity was observed particularly within categories such as Travel and Sports & Toys.
  • Fraud rates increased sharply with transaction amount. Transactions below $100 had substantially lower fraud rates, while transactions above $300 showed much higher fraud rates in this dataset.

Key Fraud-Prevention Opportunities

  • Apply additional screening and monitoring to higher-value transactions, particularly transactions above approximately $300.
  • Prioritize merchants with unusually high fraud rates, fraudulent transaction volumes, or fraudulent transaction values for investigation.
  • Strengthen monitoring within categories where significant fraudulent activity was observed, including Leisure, Travel, and Sports & Toys.
  • Use behavioral analytics to identify customers associated with repeated suspicious transaction patterns.
  • Combine transaction amount, merchant activity, category, customer behavior, and other available signals when assessing transaction risk.
  • Monitor fraud rate and fraudulent transaction value regularly to evaluate the effect of fraud-prevention measures.
